## SHEET SPEC - apply this to EVERY enemy
================================================================================

These constraints are not stylistic preferences. They are what my slicer relies
on to cut clean frames, and what stops animation bleeding between cells.

  - Transparent background (RGBA). No drop shadows, no vignette, no ground
	plane, no grid lines, no borders drawn around cells.
  - Horizontal STRIP layout: one animation per row, frames left to right.
  - All frames in ALL rows share ONE horizontal BASELINE. The soles/underside
	of the character must sit on the same imaginary ground line in every single
	frame. This is the single most important rule - a drifting baseline makes
	the character look like it is floating or sinking.
  - Leave at least 8% of the cell width as empty transparent space between
	neighbouring frames so limbs never touch and bleed in.
  - Every frame centred horizontally in its cell.
  - Character fully inside its cell - do not crop at the edges.
  - Pixel art, limited palette, hard 1px clean edges, no anti-aliasing, no
	soft glow unless the character is literally made of light.
  - The character FACES RIGHT in every frame. The game mirrors it at runtime,
	so never draw a left-facing variant.
  - Consistent scale and consistent character design across all rows of the
	sheet. Same colours, same silhouette, same costume in every frame.
  - Output as a single PNG, no transparency checkerboard baked into the image.

Recommended canvas: width 1400-1800px, one row per animation, rows stacked
vertically with clear empty bands between rows.

Frame counts are chosen to match the timing already coded in the project:
  - Basic enemy run = 12fps, attack = 14fps, hurt = 10fps, death = 8fps
  - Imp dodge window is 0.30s, so 4 frames at ~13fps
  - Knight windup is 0.28s, impact 0.07s, recovery 0.45s

## 1. CINDER IMP  (cinder_imp) - the fast dodger
================================================================================

PROMPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pixel art sprite sheet of a small mischievous fire imp, side view, facing
right, on a fully transparent background.

The imp is a fast, cowardly creature made of ember and cinder: a small round
scrappy body with two tiny legs, short pointed horns, a wicked little grin, and
a wisp of flame instead of a head plume. Its body glows hot orange-red
(around #FF7326) with darker charred-red shading and bright yellow-white
highlights on the hottest parts of its face and hands. It is small - roughly
half the height of a human - so its proportions are scrappy and slightly
chibi: big head relative to body, small limbs, energetic posture. It looks
cute and mischievous, not genuinely scary.

Rows, top to bottom, frames left to right:

  ROW 1 - IDLE, 4 frames
	Standing bounce-loop, feet planted, flame wisp flickering. Small vertical
	bob of a few pixels, alternating weight shift onto each foot. Arms relaxed.

  ROW 2 - RUN, 6 frames
	Fast sprint cycle. Lean forward, legs cycling through contact, passing,
	contact, passing. Arms pumping hard. Flame wisp streaming BACKWARD as if
	wind-blown by its own speed. This is much faster and more frantic than a
	walk - motion energy on every frame.

  ROW 3 - ATTACK, 4 frames
	Lunge-bite. Crouch and coil back, then spring forward with jaws/mouth wide
	open and both little claws thrust out. Last frame is the impact pose,
	lunging forward with a small burst of flame.

  ROW 4 - DODGE, 4 frames
	A desperate evasive BACKFLIP away from danger. Tuck and crouch, launch
	backward off the ground, rotate upside-down in mid-air with all four limbs
	splayed, then land back on its feet. This reads as a panicked escape, and
	its body should be tinted a distinct VIOLET/PURPLE (around #9959FF)
	instead of orange to sell that it is briefly intangible/unhittable.

  ROW 5 - HURT, 2 frames
	Reacting to a hit: recoil backward, eyes squeezed shut, mouth open in a
	yelp, limbs thrown out. One frame for the flinch, one for the settle.

  ROW 6 - DEATH, 5 frames
	Pop and crumble. Start standing, then burst upward into a shower of
	orange embers, leaving only ash and a fading puff of smoke that dissipates
	into nothing. Losing material as it dies.

Technical notes from the code, for accuracy:
  - Body collision box is 24x42px, so the character should read as roughly
	45-55px tall in the source art with room for the dodge arc.
  - Fastest enemy in the game (speed 175 vs the basic soul's 80). Motion should
    look frantic, not ponderous.
  - It is invulnerable DURING the dodge, which is why the dodge reads violet.
  - Dies fast (death_delay 0.4s), so the death row should read quickly.

Design ground truth:
  colours from scenes/enemies/cinder_imp.tscn -> Color(1, 0.45, 0.2)
  dodge colour from cinder_imp.gd:53-62 -> Color(0.6, 0.35, 1.0)
  dodge/attack logic from cinder_imp.gd:43-63

## 2. TEARDROP  (teardrop) - the flying projectile
================================================================================

NOTE: This one is NOT a walking character. It is a thrown projectile that
spawns in mid-air, flies at 220px/s, and despawns on contact. It has no legs
and never touches the ground, so it does NOT need a baseline or a walk cycle.
Give it a spin instead.

PROMPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pixel art sprite sheet of a single animated water droplet projectile on a
fully transparent background, facing right.

A plump glossy teardrop of water: rounded bottom, tapering to a sharp point at
the top trailing behind, like a falling drop of water. Bright luminous cyan
(around #59E6FF) with a paler near-white highlight bubble on the lower front
and a deeper blue-teal edge on the trailing side. It reads as a small energetic
water blob with a bit of spirit in it - not a glass ornament, not a rock.

Rows, top to bottom, frames left to right:

  ROW 1 - SPIN / FLY, 6 frames
    The droplet tumbling through the air as it travels. A smooth continuous
    rotation of the whole drop, exactly one full 360 degree turn across the
    6 frames. The highlight must rotate WITH the body so the spin is readable
    as rotation and not as a shape morph. Keep the silhouette a teardrop in
    every frame - only its orientation changes.

  ROW 2 - SPLASH, 3 frames
    The droplet bursts on impact. Frame 1: the body splatters outward into a
    ring of droplets and a ragged crown of water. Frame 2: the crown breaks
    into scattered separate droplets flying outward and downward. Frame 3:
    only a few tiny residual specks and mist remain, nearly gone.

Technical notes from the code:
  - Small fast projectile (collision radius 7, visual 10x16), but it must still
    be clearly VISIBLE on screen against the level backgrounds, so do not make
    it tiny in the source art - aim for roughly 40-60px tall so it scales down
    cleanly.
  - Lifetime is 3.5s, so a 6-frame spin loop reads fine at any travel speed.
  - Dies on contact with the player OR any static scenery, so the splash row
    is used for both cases.

Design ground truth:
  colour from scenes/enemies/teardrop.tscn -> Color(0.35, 0.9, 1.0)
  projectile behaviour from teardrop.gd (vel, ttl 3.5, damage 6)

## 3. TEARDROP SHOOTER  (teardrop_shooter) - the kiter
================================================================================

PROMPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pixel art sprite sheet of a tall slender water-spitter creature, side view,
facing right, on a fully transparent background.

An elegant, upright, slightly anxious-looking water elemental: a narrow
teardrop-shaped body that tapers to a point at the top of its head, pale
sky-blue / teal (around #33B3D9) with a lighter belly and darker blue shading
where it tapers. It has a single large expressive eye near the top of its body
and a small open mouth-spout low on its face that it aims at you. Two thin
stick-like legs. It should read as nervous and skittish - a creature that
wants to keep its distance rather than brawl. Taller and thinner than the basic
soul, roughly 15% taller.

Rows, top to bottom, frames left to right:

  ROW 1 - IDLE, 4 frames
    Nervous hovering-bob. Weight on one leg then the other, body bobbing up and
    down a few pixels, eye darting side to side as if checking if you are
    approaching. The tapered head sways slightly. Antsy, never fully still.

  ROW 2 - RUN, 6 frames
    Skittering walk cycle. Long thin legs cycling, body leaning into the
    direction of travel, the tall tapered head bobbing with each step. Because
    it backpedals a lot, the walk must read clearly in reverse too - keep the
    limbs distinct and separated so the cycle is legible backwards.

  ROW 3 - SHOOT, 5 frames
    Spitting a water droplet at a range. Frame 1: it inhales, cheeks/body
    swelling slightly, eye narrowing. Frame 2: it leans back and aims the
    mouth-spout directly forward. Frame 3: full recoil, mouth wide open with
    a small blue droplet being fired out in front of it. Frame 4: a follow-
    through spurt with droplets spraying. Frame 5: a brief exhausted slump,
    body wobbling, before it resets. The fired droplet should be a small
    separate blue blob just ahead of its mouth.

  ROW 4 - RETREAT, 4 frames
    Backing away defensively. Leaning away from the threat with both arms and
    legs braced backwards, spraying a small defensive burst of water droplets
    outward as a barrier, body tilted well away from centre. Reads as
	"go away, I don't want to fight you."

  ROW 5 - HURT, 2 frames
    Flinching from a hit: body jackknifes sideways, eye squeezed shut, water
    scattering off it in droplets.

  ROW 6 - DEATH, 5 frames
    Dehydrating and evaporating. Body deflates and sinks, colour draining from
    saturated teal to pale washed-out blue, then breaks into a spray of small
    droplets that fall and evaporate, leaving a damp patch that fades out.

Technical notes from the code:
  - Body collision box is 30x50px, so target roughly 60-70px of character
    height in the source art.
  - It is a KITER: it retreats if you get closer than 260px and only closes to
    about 620px to fire. So its body language should favour backing away -
    that is the core of its character.
  - Fires every 1.6s, so the shoot animation should be quick and repeatable.

Design ground truth:
  colour from scenes/enemies/teardrop_shooter.tscn -> Color(0.2, 0.7, 0.85)
  kiting logic from teardrop_shooter.gd:32-40 (MIN_RANGE 260, FIRE_RANGE 620)

## 4. SORROW KNIGHT  (sorrow_knight) - the telegraph boss
================================================================================

THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT ONE TO GET RIGHT.

This enemy is a timed duellist, not a brawler. The code runs an explicit state
machine - WINDUP (0.28s) -> ACTIVE impact (0.07s) -> RECOVERY (0.45s) - and the
player's ONLY way to beat it is to read the windup tell and react. So the
anticipation pose must be instantly readable at a glance, and the recovery
slump must be obviously helpless. The artwork IS the difficulty setting here.

PROMPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pixel art sprite sheet of a tall, imposing armoured knight consumed by grief,
side view, facing right, on a fully transparent background.

A heavy, slow, mournful knight: bulky dark plate armour stained with dull
purple (around #8C4DA6), a dented breastplate, a tattered cape hanging behind
that drags toward the ground, and a simple helm with a single narrow glowing
visor slit instead of a face. The silhouette is broad and top-heavy with very
thick arms and legs, making it look slow and immovable. It should read as sad
and tired rather than angry - a warrior who is exhausted and fights out of
obligation. This is the largest enemy in the game, noticeably bigger and
bulkier than a normal enemy.

Rows, top to bottom, frames left to right:

  ROW 1 - IDLE, 4 frames
    Standing vigil, head bowed. Slow heavy breathing: the whole body rises and
    falls a few pixels, the cape shifts, the visor glow pulses gently. Weight
    shifting slightly between the legs. A mournful resting pose.

  ROW 2 - WALK, 6 frames
    Slow deliberate march. Heavy ponderous steps, each footfall planting
    firmly before the other lifts, the body rocking slightly with the weight
    transfer. The cape drags and sways behind with real delay. Everything
    should look heavy and reluctant - no bounce, no energy.

  ROW 3 - GUARD, 2 frames
    Raising the shield/arm to block, triggered when the player attacks. Braces
    into a defensive crouch behind a raised guard, body angled away, cape
	pulled in tight, visor dimming. Reads as "this hit will not land."

  ROW 4 - WINDUP, 3 frames
    THE CRITICAL TELEGRAPH. The knight coils BACKWARD before striking: the
    weapon arm pulls back and up behind the body, the torso leans away from
    the target, and the body visibly compresses and stretches TALL - it
    narrows horizontally and grows vertically as it loads the swing. Progress
    across the 3 frames from a small coil to a maximum extreme coil-back, so
    the anticipation is unmistakable. Add a bright amber/orange energy
    gathering at the weapon tip that intensifies each frame. This pose must be
	readable as "I am about to hit you very hard."

  ROW 5 - SWING, 3 frames
    The strike itself. Frame 1: explosive forward swing, body lunging hard in
    the direction it faces, weapon sweeping down and across in a wide arc.
    Frame 2: full IMPACT - maximum forward extension, body stretched wide and
    squashed vertically, weapon at the far end of the arc, bright white flash
    at the point of contact. Frame 3: hold the end of the arc, still extended.
    This is a fast violent change from the windup row - the contrast is the
    whole point.

  ROW 6 - RECOVERY, 3 frames
    THE PUNISH WINDOW. The knight is committed and helpless. The weapon stays
    low and forward, the body slumps downward, shoulders dropping, head
    lowering, stance widening and going soft. Progressively more defeated and
    more slumped across the 3 frames, ending clearly open and vulnerable. The
    armour desaturates toward a tired grey-purple as it loses its glow. This
	pose must read unmistakably as "safe to hit me now."

  ROW 7 - HURT, 2 frames
    Taking a hit while idle: a heavy full-body flinch, head snapping back,
    armour rattling, cape jerking.

  ROW 8 - DEATH, 6 frames
    A slow, heavy, dignified collapse - it does not pop or explode. Frame 1:
    staggers backward, knees buckling. Frame 2: drops to one knee. Frame 3:
    sinks further, one hand on the ground. Frame 4: falls forward onto both
    hands. Frame 5: collapses flat, the cape settling over it. Frame 6: fully
    prone and still, visor light fading to black, armour dulled and scorched.
    Slow, weighted, and sad rather than violent.

Technical notes from the code - these mirror real runtime transforms:
  - Body collision box is 50x92px, the biggest in the game. Target roughly
    100-115px of character height in the source art.
  - Windup scale is animated to (1.0 - 0.10t, 1.0 + 0.12t): it gets NARROWER
    and TALLER as it charges. Draw the windup frames progressively thinner and
    taller to match.
  - Impact scale is (1.25, 0.78): WIDER and SHORTER on the hit frame. Draw the
    impact frame visibly broader and compressed.
  - Recovery scale is (1.0 + 0.12s, 1.0 - 0.12s): it slowly slumps wider and
    shorter.
  - Windup 0.28s, impact 0.07s, recovery 0.45s. The recovery is the longest
    window in the game, so make it read as clearly and unmistakably vulnerable.
  - The windup tell pulses faster as it nears the strike. Suggest this in the
    artwork by making the glow build across the 3 windup frames.
  - Colour language from the code: idle purple, guard GREEN, windup builds to
    AMBER then hot yellow, impact WHITE, recovery desaturates to grey. Bake
    these tints into the relevant rows so the pose and the colour tell agree.
  - speed is only 42, so it should always look like it is barely moving.

Design ground truth:
  colours from sorrow_knight.gd:23-38 (BASE_PURPLE, BLOCK_GREEN, WARN_AMBER,
  WARN_HOT, IMPACT_WHITE, RECOVERY_GREY)
  squash/stretch from sorrow_knight.gd:166-192 (_update_visual)
  timings from sorrow_knight.gd:16-22
  damage 12, hp 120 from sorrow_knight.gd:42,61

  They are unreliable at frame-to-frame consistency. If a generated sheet comes
  back with a character that changes shape or colour between frames, the clean
   fallback is to generate ONE strong standing pose per animation row and I will
   build the in-between motion with code-driven squash/stretch tweening, the
   same way the sorrow knight already does it. Tell me if you want that route
   instead.
